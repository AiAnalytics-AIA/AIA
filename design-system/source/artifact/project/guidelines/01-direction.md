# Brand direction and the trade-off

## Recommended: A — Instrument

Graphite and paper, one cold calibrated accent (`signal`, a blue with a cyan lean), and one warm counterpart (`status-you`, amber) reserved for *waiting on a person*.

Why A wins:

- **It gives the refusals a vocabulary.** An instrument panel already has a grammar for "live", "held", "fault" and "needs an operator". AIA's parked states map onto it directly. A dashboard grammar would map them onto "pending" grey and lose the difference.
- **It keeps colour available for meaning.** With a near-achromatic base, the few hues left (signal, amber, fault red and the data palette) are all information. Every added decorative hue would make amber less loud.
- **It holds density.** Plex Sans has tabular figures by default, a large x-height and unambiguous 1/l/I and 0/O at 13px, including with Czech diacritics. This matters most in the ledger and the respondent explorer.
- **It is honest about the product.** A lattice of points is literally what the population is. It needs no metaphor, no "AI" imagery and no national symbolism.

## Alternate pass: B — Editorial

Shown as the *DirectionB* screen, on the Portfolio. Warmer paper and Source Serif headings and table text.

- Stronger: the report and the cover, where B's voice is already used — the Deliverable register *is* direction B.
- Weaker: dense tables. Serif figures lose their rhythm in a 36px row, háčky over ř and č merge at 13px, and warm paper cuts the gap between the amber state and the ground. The parked row is the one thing that must be loud, and B quietens it.

Decision: **A for the Studio, B's typography for the Deliverable.** They share one token set, so the report and the tool read as one company.

## Not pursued: C — Lattice

Using the synthetic population as the entire identity (the field everywhere, as texture) is strong on the cover and weak everywhere else. A field of dots behind a table is noise on the pixels a conclusion rests on. The motif is kept at the scale where it works: the mark, empty states, dividers, the report cover and Sociomapa's grammar.

## The trade-off, stated plainly

This system optimises for **five experts over eight-hour days** and for **epistemic honesty over visual reassurance**. It will look denser and more sober than a product built to impress a first-time visitor. Some states are deliberately loud in a way a consumer product would soften: a parked-on-you row is solid amber; *chybí* is drawn heavier than a number; a report says "Externí prediktivní validace dosud neproběhla" in a boxed statement, not in a footnote. That is the accepted cost.

**If AIA ever gains a client-facing surface**, change these and nothing else:

1. Give clients a read-only Deliverable register (a web report), never the Studio. The Studio's density and loud states are for operators.
2. Replace amber *waiting on you* with plain "in preparation" copy. Clients never owe AIA a decision in the product, so the amber state would be meaningless to them.
3. Keep every evidence grade, the *chybí* treatment and the holdout statement. These are the product's honesty and must not be softened for a paying audience.
4. Take the client accent out of the chrome. A client should see their own brand or none; the accent exists to stop *researchers* confusing two clients.
