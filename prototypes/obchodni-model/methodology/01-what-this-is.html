Before the chapters

What this is

The method AIA follows when a client must choose between options with large sums at stake: a set of sites, suppliers, investments or keeping the current arrangement. It is the measure the MVP is held to.

What the method produces

A decision between options, judged by a rule the client wrote before any result, on a cost model computed by code, with the uncertainty of every input carried through to the verdict. The report says what the rule chooses, how far that verdict is from changing, what information would change it, and what each option means for finance, operations and the schedule. Two maps show how the options relate across the model's futures and across the client's weightings of the business perspectives; they inform, the rule decides.

Studies without a model (a situation report) and studies built on survey respondents are not this method. The survey lane is frozen (ADR 0046, decision 2).

How to read it

  • Chapter 1 is the spine: the eight steps in one table.
  • Chapter 2 is the analytical contract: the defaults every step runs under until the methodologist amends them, and the two release states.
  • Chapters 3 to 10 take one step each, in the same six parts: what goes in, what comes out, the checks, what AIA has today, what is new, and what the acceptance test expects.
  • Chapter 11 says what is built and what is designed. No code for this study type is on develop.
  • Chapters 12 to 14 cross the steps: the business perspectives and the two maps; grades, sources and the release gate; governance and acceptance.
  • The appendix is a glossary.

Czech words in italics are terms a person sees on AIA's screens or in the report. Words in code are files, modules or plan chunks in the repository. "The review" is the product owner's methodology review of 5 November 2026 (ADR 0046 § The methodology review). "The scope" is MVP-SCOPE.md; where the two documents disagree, the scope wins. "The owner" is the product owner; "the methodologist" is the methodology owner.

Three things this document does not do. It does not invent a number: the numbers in it are rules, clearly illustrative values, or the toy acceptance fixtures of chapter 14. It does not decide what waits on the owner or the methodologist; chapter 14 lists those items. It does not describe how a screen works: the design boards do that.
