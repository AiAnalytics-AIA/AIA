Chapter 11

Perspectives, components and the two maps

The owner asked for every business perspective: financial, strategic, operational, marketing, logistics, "just everything". A perspective is a set of components of the decision; a component is one measure per option with a source, a grade and a direction. The components feed one new map, the weighting-scenario map, which sits beside the futures map and is never merged with it. This chapter takes its definitions and ChemVitro's components from the scope, § 3 and § 4.

Perspectives and components

A component is one measure per option, with a source (a model result, a client-data number, a cited finding, or a named expert's score), a grade (one of the exemplar's seven, chapter 12) and a direction (↑ higher is better, ↓ lower is better). A perspective is a set of components. The weighting-scenario map scores components; the client's rule does not read them.

Five rules, from the review of 5 November:

  • Gates are not components (R2). The rule's four conditions are pass / fail / unknown per option. An unknown gate is "cannot be recommended until evidenced", and no weight compensates a failed one.
  • A missing component keeps a range, never a score (R2). The cell shows the range it could take; the map is drawn at the documented minimum with the range beside it.
  • One mechanism, one component (R3, "no double counting"). A component whose mechanism the model's saving already holds is marked v modelu; one the model does not hold is mimo model. A scenario may weight the saving or its parts; the overlap check (§ 11.3, line 6) refuses a vector that weights both.
  • Judgement is labelled (A3). A component scored by a person carries that person's name and the grade úsudek.
  • An empty perspective stays and says so. For ChemVitro the marketing perspective is empty; for Velis it is central.

What the table shows for the map: 22 scored components, of which 18 are v modelu. A scenario that weights "finance 0,5 · provoz 0,3 · logistika 0,2" over these scores operations and logistics twice, because the saving already holds their cost per tonne. Two honest scenario families exist: (a) the saving against what the model leaves out (shared failure, hiring time, hiring competition), and (b) the parts of the saving against each other (people, maintenance, transport, permitting cost) without the saving itself. The map records which family a scenario belongs to. Which family the owner's example means is his open decision (chapter 13).

The futures map

Chapter 8 describes it. In one line each: rows are the model's draws; the claim is about the model; the cell is the rule's measure; centring per future makes the reading relative; the uncentred shared-failure table reads the absolute; links only where the mode contract allows; a stability test over settings declared in advance; relative apart from absolute; internal until the futures mode has its register entry.

The weighting-scenario map (Mapa scénářů vah, board MV9)

The owner's request: "allocate different weights to different components … whether logistics is a 0,3 and operations management is 0,5 … run the scenarios and display the scenarios on the Sociomaps." The ten lines of its contract, as the scope writes them (§ 4; chunk sociomapa-systemu#SYS13, new):

  1. Objects are the options: the eight sites. The buy baseline is the rule's reference, not an object.
  2. Rows are named weight vectors over perspectives or components, composed by the researcher or the client's people ("FR: finance 0,5 · provoz 0,3 · logistika 0,2"), each with who composed it and when, recorded in the approval ledger before results, by a composer who has not seen them (swing weighting, R3).
  3. The fan around each row is k perturbed vectors from a declared concentration; it is a sensitivity setting, never a probability (R4). The map prints podíl testovaných nastavení, v nichž je možnost první, never P.
  4. A cell is the option's weighted score: each component scaled on its declared extremes and direction (cost-model-study#CS1), gates excluded and shown as pass / fail / unknown beside the map (R2).
  5. A missing component keeps its range: the cell is an interval, the map is drawn at the documented minimum, and the row says what the top of the range would change (R2).
  6. The overlap check marks components with one mechanism (§ 11.1, v modelu); a vector that weights the saving and one of its parts is refused by name and offered the two honest families.
  7. Height is the weighted score under the scenario the reader chooses; relative (centred per row) and absolute readings side by side, as MV3's contract for the scenario mode (R5).
  8. Layout is the aia-sociomap-5 engine's over the rows × objects table, as the prototype already runs it (tools/engine_maps.py, compute_object_map); its pair statistics are not read, because the rows are settings we chose (MV3). Options close together win under the same weightings.
  9. Apart from the futures map. The futures map's rows are the model's draws; this map's rows are preferences. They sit side by side and are never merged.
  10. Where it goes. The map is preference information. It enters the report under Co by závěr změnilo, never the recommendation; the client's rule decides (R3). Its method is a Metodika register entry of its own (sociomapa-systemu#SYS12), internal until the methodologist approves it, labelled metoda čeká na schválení metodikem wherever it appears.

What the exemplar shows for it

The exemplar has no weighting map; it has the material for one. Its components per site are in T1, T4, T5, T6, T8 and T9, and its text already weighs them in words: the Czech and Polish sites win on finance and operations, Wendelstedt on the schedule, the German sites on permitting certainty, and none on all. A family (a) map would show that the three leaders win under every weighting that keeps the saving first, and that only a heavy weight on shared failure or hiring competition moves Kamenec and Odrzyca apart; a family (b) map would show where the German sites lose (people, maintenance, transport) and where they do not (permitting cost). Wendelstedt's deadline certainty is a gate, not a weight, and the gate is what keeps it out. That is the kind of statement the map may make: what the preferences would favour, not what the rule chooses.
