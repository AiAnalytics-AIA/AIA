Chapter 11

Perspectives, components and the two maps

The owner asked for every business perspective: financial, strategic, operational, marketing, logistics, "just everything". A perspective is not a view of the results. It is a set of components of the decision, each a measure per option with a source and a direction. The components feed one new map, the weighting-scenario map, which sits beside the futures map and is never merged with it.

Perspectives and components

A component is one measure per option with: a source (a model result, a client datum, a cited finding, or a named expert's score); a direction (higher is better, or lower is); a value scale with declared extremes, so that the component can be read on 0–1; and a state per option (known, unknown, not applicable, evidenced absent or present; review R2). A perspective groups components. A perspective with no component for a case stays empty and says so.

The futures map

Chapter 8 describes it. In one line each: rows are futures; the claim is about the model; the cell is the rule's measure; centring per future makes the reading relative; the uncentred shared-failure table reads the absolute; links only where the mode contract allows; a stability test over settings declared in advance; relative apart from absolute; internal until the futures mode is registered.

The weighting-scenario map (Mapa scénářů vah, board MV9)

The owner's request: "allocate different weights to different components … whether logistics is a 0,3 and operations management is 0,5 … run the scenarios and display the scenarios on the Sociomaps."

Rows
Scenarios: named weight vectors over the components or the perspectives, composed by the researcher or the client's people. Example names, to be replaced by the case's own: FR: finance 0,5 · provoz 0,3 · logistika 0,2; EHS: povolování 0,5 · prostředí 0,3 · finance 0,2; Rovné váhy. Around each named scenario a fan: perturbed weight vectors within a declared distance, renormalised, so that a scenario is a region, not a point.
Objects
The options (the eight sites; the baseline where it has components).
Cell
The option's weighted score under that weight vector: each component read on its 0–1 value scale in its direction, multiplied by its weight, summed.
Height
The weighted score under the scenario chosen for display.
Distance
Options close together score alike across the tested weightings: they win or lose under the same preferences.
Statistics
None of the engine's pair statistics are read: the rows are settings we chose, not a sample (board MV3's scenario contract; README "Known wrong"). Instead, shares of tested settings: for each option, the share of the fan's settings in which it ranks first (review R4). A share is a share of tested settings, never a probability.

The rules the map keeps

  • The client's rule decides. The map is preference information. It goes to the report's part 3, Co by závěr změnilo, and to the appendix, never into the recommendation (review R3; K3).
  • Gates are never compensated. The rule's four conditions and any non-compensable requirement are gates: an option that fails one cannot rank first under any weighting; an option whose gate is unknown "cannot be recommended until evidenced" (review R2). The map shows gated options apart or marks them.
  • A missing component keeps a range, never a score. An option with an unknown component is scored as an interval over the component's possible values; the share counts the settings in which it ranks first under its worst value and under its best, and the map prints the coverage of each option's weights.
  • Weights come from the decision-maker's trade-offs (swing weighting), approved with the value scales before any score is read; other stakeholders' weights are a sensitivity test (review R3).
  • Two maps, never one. The weighting map and the futures map answer different questions (preferences; shared risks in the model). They are shown side by side with their own contracts and are not merged, as the respondent and model maps are not (ADR 0046).
  • The engine is reused, the statistics are not. The layout and terrain of aia-sociomap-5 draw the map; the split-half and the pair tests are not printed for this mode. The map is internal until the indicator method v2 (states, gates, swing weights, value scales) has a register entry (chapter 13).

What the exemplar shows for it

The exemplar has no weighting map; it has the material for one. Its components per site are in T1, T4, T5, T6 and T9, and its text already weighs them in words: the Czech and Polish sites win on finance and operations, Wendelstedt on the schedule, the German sites on permitting certainty, and none on all. A weighting map would show that the three leaders win under every finance-heavy weighting, that Wendelstedt wins only when the deadline condition is read as a weight rather than a gate, and that the gate is what keeps it out. That is exactly the kind of statement the map may make: what the preferences would favour, not what the rule chooses.
