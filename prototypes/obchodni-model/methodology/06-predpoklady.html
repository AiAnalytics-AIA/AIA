Chapter 5 · Step 4

Předpoklady: one table of inputs

Every input of the model is one row of one table: its low, likely and high value, its distribution, its source, its grade, the person who stands behind it and its state. The table is the contract between the data and the model, and the report prints it whole.

What goes in

  • The data contracts and the findings table from step 2; the typed public inputs from step 3; the client's answers from step 1.
  • The comparable cases the client holds, from which ranges can be estimated rather than judged: for ChemVitro, 36 comparable plants with their overrun, delay and what each built beyond the plant.
  • The scope statement of every estimate the client supplies: what the EPC contractor's figure includes and excludes (epc_odhad.pdf p. 3).

What comes out

The input table, the exemplar's T9 (R2-P18). Its columns are the method:

Beside the table: the correlation record (which inputs move together, with what coefficient, why, and the stability test their judgement will be put to), the scope check of every supplied estimate against each option's documents with the exclusions priced, and the stress cases outside the ranges, kept apart from the futures (board M2 § 04).

Checks

  • Where the client's data allows an estimate, the range comes from the data, not from a judgement; the row says which. The exemplar: overrun and delay from the comparable plants, not from the estimator's ±25 % (R2-P05).
  • Two things that happened together in the data are drawn together in the model. Overrun and delay have a Spearman correlation of 0,89 across the comparable plants, so each future draws one real plant and takes both (R2-P05).
  • Every supplied estimate is checked for scope against each option's documents, and each exclusion is priced or stated as missing (R2-P05, fourth assumption; answer key § 3: a study that adds nothing to the EPC figure has missed all three traps).
  • A decisive input has an uncertainty type: natural variation, parameter, expert disagreement, model structure, or a severe event outside the range; the last goes to a stress test, not into the distribution (review R6; board M2 § 02).
  • Experts who disagree from the same documents are a mixture that does not narrow the range; the report says they differ in reading, not in facts (board M2).
  • A correlation that is a judgement is declared as one, with the alternative settings it will be tested under, before anything is computed (R2-P05 fifth assumption, Appendix B).
  • No input the files do not hold is given a point value. It gets a range and the grade úsudek; the exemplar has 22 such inputs (R2-P18 Appendix B).

How the exemplar did it

Chapter 4 (R2-P05) names the two assumptions the verdict rests on that no document supports: the 20 % chance that an authority imposes compensation measures where the station is under the limits, and the terms of purchase after 2035. Then three more: overrun and delay from 36 comparable plants (median overrun 17 %, 90th percentile 30,2 %, maximum 61 %; delay median 3 months, maximum 15), drawn jointly; the exclusions of the EPC estimate priced per site from the site's documents (18 ha of land everywhere; the bridge at Eschfeld 3,2–5,0 mil. EUR; the spur at Odrzyca 3,4 km at 1,9–2,8 mil. EUR per km; pre-treatment at Lipová and Lindenau 6,7–14,1 mil. EUR; compensation 3–15 mil. EUR where imposed; the park fee at Wendelstedt 1,1 mil. EUR a year); and the energy price correlations as judgement (0,8 electricity between countries, 0,6 electricity–gas, 0,4 gas–allowance), with the stability test declared in Appendix B. The base case already carries the median overrun. The report's text says the model has 54 inputs and that 16 of 54 are public; the table T9 has 67 rows (29 ověřeno, 16 prozatímní, 22 úsudek). Session B counted from T9 and recorded the difference on board M2 § 01b as a finding for the report; it is the kind of contradiction between text and table that K6 exists to catch, and this document counts from T9.
