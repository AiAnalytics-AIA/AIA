Chapter 6 · Step 5

Model: one computation for every option

The model computes the measure the rule reads for every option in the same futures. It is code, pinned by version and seed, and a rerun gives the same bytes. Nothing in it is a forecast or a legal opinion.

What goes in

  • The input table of step 4, frozen as a revision; the options and the baseline of step 1; the rule's measure.
  • The timeline every option shares: for ChemVitro, decision and permit applications on 1. 2. 2027, permitting 8–11 months plus the site's extensions, construction 22 months plus the drawn comparable plant's delay, ramp-up at 60–90 % for six months (Appendix B).
  • The seed and the number of futures, or a stopping rule that sets the number.

What comes out

  • The base case: one computation with every input at its likely value. It compares the options and feeds the sensitivity analyses; it is not an estimate of what the client will save (R2-P10).
  • The futures: N seeded draws of all inputs at once, with their correlations, each future computing the baseline and every option. From them: the median, the 90 % interval, and every probability as a share of futures (R2-P04 § 3.3).
  • The extra computations on the same model and the same futures, each changing one assumption: the decision boundary over an assumption's range, delay of the decision, hiring delay, alternative terms, correlation settings (Appendix B).
  • The run record: model type and version, input revision, seed, N, the methods pinned, the artefacts with their SHA-256 (MV0 M-2, M-3).

Checks

  • Every option is computed in the same futures by the same model; the baseline is one of them (R2-P04 § 3.3).
  • The same inputs and seed give the same bytes (M-3). This verifies the implementation, not the model's fitness for the decision (review R1; chapter 13).
  • The number of futures follows a stopping rule tied to the decision: the verdict of each condition is known to within a stated margin (board M2: ±0,5 pp on the pass probabilities) or N is declared and justified. The exemplar declares N = 20 000 (R2-P04 § 3.3).
  • Cash flows are discounted at the rule's rate, nominal or real as declared, with the convention named (mid-year, inflation 2 % provisional; Appendix B).
  • Tax is inside the model as a fixed parameter per country, so the after-tax view agrees with every other view (review I4). The rule reads the pre-tax figure.
  • What is outside the model is listed where the model is described, not discovered in the limits (R2-P15: transfer prices, hiring time in the schedule, raw-material freight, the annual step).
  • An extra computation changes one assumption; combinations that are not computed are said not to be (R2-P15).

How the exemplar did it

The cost of supply is the investment, operations from start-up, VTX-40 bought until start-up, purchases above 40 000 t, less a residual value of 30 % of the investment in 2044; the saving of a site is the baseline's cost less the site's, in NPV, positive when the plant is cheaper (R2-P04 § 3.3). Purchases follow the supplier's formula to 30. 6. 2030, then the renewal premium, or the spot price while the plant is late (Appendix B). Operations are six items per tonne: raw materials at 540 EUR/t delivered, electricity and gas at the sites' offers moving with the country's price path, CO₂ at the allowance price less free allocation, water, sewer, hazardous waste and product freight at the quotes, 95 staff at the operator wage times a wage mix and contributions, maintenance and insurance at 2–4 % of the EPC estimate (Appendix B). The futures number 20 000 with seed 20270201. The base case carries the median overrun of 17 %; the futures draw one comparable plant for overrun and delay together. Eight extra computations reuse the same futures, each changing one assumption (Appendix B).
