Chapter 3 · Step 1

Zadání: the decision and its rule

The study begins by writing down what will be decided, between which options, under which constraints, by whom and when, and by which rule. The rule is frozen before anyone sees a comparison of the options.

What goes in

  • The client's brief, as typed or pasted: one field of free text and the files that come with it.
  • The decision-maker, the date the decision is taken, and the date the study must be ready.
  • The options, each with the client's source for it, and a baseline every option is compared with (often the status quo: keep doing what the client does now).
  • The constraints every option must meet: an investment ceiling, a start date, a capacity.
  • The client's answers to the study's questions. Where an answer is "we don't know", the input becomes a judgement with a range and says so.

What comes out

  • The decision record: what is decided, why now, the goal, who decides, by when.
  • The options and the baseline, named as the client names them, with the source of each.
  • The rule, frozen and dated, in the client's words: the measure it reads, the choice it makes, and each condition with its threshold. A typical rule: choose the option with the highest median saving against the baseline, measured as the present value of the cost of supply over the horizon at a stated rate, if the probability that it beats the baseline, the probability of a loss above a stated sum and the probability of starting by a stated date each meet their thresholds, and its investment stays under the ceiling; else recommend nothing and name the assumption most worth checking.
  • Interpretation records. Where a condition does not say how it is measured, the study writes its reading down, the alternative reading, what each would change, and who confirms it. A ceiling with no probability attached, for example, can be judged on the base case or "in every future"; the record says which, prices the other, and names the person who confirms.
  • The views the client asked for beside the rule: after-tax values, the verdict at other rates. They are shown; the rule does not read them.
  • The analyses the report will carry and the perspectives that apply (chapter 12).

Checks

  • The rule exists before the first comparative result, and the freeze is dated and attributed. Reading the client's files at intake is feasibility work and is recorded as such.
  • Every condition has a measure the model can compute and a threshold. A condition without one gets an interpretation record, never a silent default.
  • An amendment of the rule after the freeze is approved by the decision-maker, dated, and printed in the report.
  • Every option is described comparably: the same scope, the same horizon, the same discount rate.
  • The brief is Class B material by rule (domain/material_rules.py); a language model may read it. A client's dataset is Class A and is not sent to a model.

New

  • The business Zadání inside the one merged study, with no new study kind (cost-model-study#CP3): the eight parts (decision-brief#DB0), triage as a Lead turn (#DB2), the decision core and the rule (#DB3), readiness (#DB4), the hand-off naming the files each step takes (#DB5).
  • The rule as a typed object of n conditions, each with its own threshold and, where stated, a probability, judged per option at the stated rate and two alternates, frozen as a Design Revision (decision-brief#DB3b, new, extending cost-model-study#CR7, which judges one quantity against one threshold). The screen shows each condition with its threshold and what it reads, the gates that no saving outweighs, and the lock line: frozen on, amendments, interpretations, awaiting confirmation.
  • The interpretation record a named person confirms: what the rule does not say, the chosen reading, the other reading and its effect, who wrote it and who confirms, where the report prints it (decision-brief#DB3b).
  • The model's parts as data: the target quantity, the baseline, the futures, the shared timeline, what is in and outside the model, the investment ceiling as a gate, the result views the model will feed, and the perspectives that have components in the study.
  • The perspectives chosen for the study (chapter 12), each with its leaves, in Zadání rather than in the model.

Acceptance

  • The test case's rule is captured with every condition and its "recommend nothing" clause, dated before any result.
  • Every condition that can be read two ways has an interpretation record naming who confirms it.
  • The baseline is the status quo at its stated terms, not one of the options.
  • The study asks the client only what the test case answers; anything else is a judgement with a range, labelled úsudek.
