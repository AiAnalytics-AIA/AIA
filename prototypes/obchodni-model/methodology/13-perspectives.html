Chapter 12

Perspectives, the cost partition and the two maps

The owner asked for every business perspective: financial, strategic, operational, marketing, logistics, "just everything", with weights on them run as scenarios and shown on the Sociomap. Version 4 gives that request one construction: every pre-tax cash-flow line of an option is allocated to exactly one leaf under exactly one perspective, a perspective's total is the discounted cost of its leaves, and one value function and one weight vector score every option alike. Overlap is impossible by construction, and the owner's "logistics 0,3, operations 0,5" means exactly what it says.

The partition

A leaf is one cash-flow line (or a group of lines that always travel together), discounted at the rule's rate. A perspective is a set of leaves. The same expenditure never appears in two leaves; distinct expenditures caused by the same event may. Delay costs enter through the purchases they actually cause, not as a separate penalty. Three checks hold the allocation: every expenditure the case documents is allocated once (the answer key's air abatement and spur land included); the perspective totals sum to the site's cost of supply; buying cost less site cost equals the NPV saving. A cost that cannot be interpreted comparably across sites (a one-off the documents give for one site only, with no counterpart elsewhere) is not admitted as a leaf; it keeps its range in the model and appears in the report as an exclusion.

Five rules carry over from the review of 5 November, two of them changed by this construction:

  • Gates are not leaves (R2). The rule's four conditions are pass / fail / unknown per option, shown beside the map, never weighted.
  • A range stays a range (R2). A leaf the files give as a range (the spur per kilometre, pre-treatment, the permitting extension) has a lower and an upper score bound, the direction applied correctly: for a cost the minimum raw value is the favourable end, so the map never draws it as the base. Both bound views are shown and labelled as bounds, not as jointly feasible futures. Nothing fills a missing fact: Bory's nearby hiring is 120 people because the client's file says so (trh_prace_regiony.xlsx, PL2), not a range.
  • One expenditure, one leaf (R3). The partition replaces version 3's three roles (úspora, v modelu, mimo model) and its two scenario families. NPV saving, after-tax NPV, IRR and payback are printed beside the weighted score, unweighted, because they already contain every leaf.
  • Judgement is labelled (A3). A leaf whose value is a person's judgement carries that person's name and the grade úsudek.
  • An empty perspective stays and says why; a globally inactive perspective has weight zero for every site, approved as such. A nonzero weight on an empty perspective is refused with the reason; nothing is redistributed silently.

One scoring structure for every option

For a site i and a perspective p, Cip is the discounted sum of p's leaves. The approved value function vp maps it onto 0–10 on fixed anchors shared by all eight sites; a value outside the anchors triggers a review, never silent clipping. The weighted score is

Si(w) = Σp wp · vp(Cip)

  • One weight vector for every site. No site loses a weight because a leaf does not apply to it: evidenced absence of an applicable expenditure is a cost of zero through the common scale; unknown expenditure is a range with two bounds. Version 3's rule that a null component "takes no weight there" is withdrawn: it compared sites under different trade-offs.
  • Leaves are an accounting breakdown, not equally weighted subcriteria. Splitting a cost line (freight into rail and road) leaves the perspective total, the score and the rank unchanged. This is the admission test of every allocation, and version 3's "a perspective's weight shared equally by its admitted components" fails it.
  • Anchors and swing weights are approved together, before any site score is shown (swing weighting, R3): a weight is a trade-off over a stated swing of a perspective's total, not a feeling about a category name. The additive form itself is reviewed with them.
  • A scenario is a named weight vector the client's people approve; the researcher records it with its scales through the existing approval flow (the owner, 9 November, R2-Q06), with who composed it and when, before results.

The owner's example, with the remaining weight assigned to finance: finance 0,20 · strategie 0 · provoz 0,50 · marketing 0 · logistika 0,30 · prostředí 0 · lidé 0. "Finance" is its partition, the capital, land and purchases, not the total NPV. The NPV still includes every cost, including the perspectives given zero diagnostic weight. The scenario is admitted; version 3's refusal of "finance 0,5 · provoz 0,3 · logistika 0,2" was a refusal of the three-role mix, which no longer exists.

Ranges and sensitivity

A ranged leaf gives the site an interval of scores. A robust leader needs a validated positive lower bound on its score difference against every rival over all admitted unknowns jointly; overlapping intervals mean leadership is not established, and the map says so. For each named scenario the study tests, in this order and reported separately:

  1. the approved centre;
  2. Dirichlet fans at concentrations 20, 60 and 180, 300 settings each, drawn on the positive-weight coordinates only so an explicit zero stays zero;
  3. directed weight transfers between pairs of perspectives, and a simplex grid where the number of active perspectives keeps it small;
  4. the approved alternative anchors and the uncertain cost allocations;
  5. the evidence bounds, coherent joint stress cases and simultaneous unknowns.

Results are shares of tested settings, per scenario, per fan and per evidence setting: the share in which an option is uniquely first, the share of ties and the share left unresolved by the bounds, which together reconcile to 100 %. Scenarios are never pooled into one popularity figure; version 3's "all tested settings: Odrzyca 35 %" is withdrawn with the families.

The Sociomap view of a scenario

Each scenario has its own view on the aia-sociomap-5 engine, under the mode contract the methodologist registers for it (sociomapa-systemu#SYS12, SYS13):

  • rows are the tested weight settings; objects are the eight sites; proximity is similarity of score response across the settings; height is the score under the selected setting;
  • a ranged leaf keeps its paired bound views and score intervals in the table beside the map;
  • the gate status of every site (pass / fail / unknown) is printed beside it; a hypothetical change of a gate's state is a named scenario, never an edit;
  • the rule's verdict stands in a panel of its own; the map is preference information under Co by závěr změnilo (R3, R10) and never the recommendation;
  • no respondent labels, no "reliable pairs", no respondent segments and no sampling-error circles appear: those belong to the respondent mode, whose statistics this mode does not read;
  • the view records its layout fit, its numerical convergence and its sensitivity under the mode contract; geometry the contract does not support produces no spatial claim.

The futures map, in one paragraph

Chapter 9 describes it. Rows are the model's draws, each centred on the mean of the options, so the map reads relative performance; heights are the uncentred mean savings; the absolute question, which options fail together, is answered by the shared-loss matrix with its denominators (T8), not by the map. Layout convergence, representation fit and robustness to the declared assumption settings are three different things and are reported apart. A grouping the report keeps after a failed global stability test (the German valley, the Czech–Polish ridge) is kept only if it is tested on its own; otherwise the relation matrix is printed without a spatial reading. "No site is a backup of another" is a claim about tested downside dependence between the focal sites and the rest; it says nothing about operational switching, which needs its own time, cost and feasibility assessment.
