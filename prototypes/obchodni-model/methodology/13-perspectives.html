Chapter 12

Perspectives, the cost partition and the two maps

The owner asked for every business perspective: financial, strategic, operational, marketing, logistics, "just everything", with weights on them run as scenarios and shown on the Sociomap. The method gives that request one construction: every pre-tax cash-flow line of an option is allocated to exactly one leaf under exactly one perspective, a perspective's total is the discounted cost of its leaves, and one value function and one weight vector score every option alike. Overlap is impossible by construction, and "logistics 0,3, operations 0,5" means exactly what it says.

The partition

A leaf is one cash-flow line (or a group of lines that always travel together), discounted at the rule's rate. A perspective is a set of leaves. The same expenditure never appears in two leaves; distinct expenditures caused by the same event may. Delay costs enter through the purchases they actually cause, not as a separate penalty. The allocation for a cost-of-supply study:

PerspectiveLeaves (each a cash-flow line, discounted)

Strategickáno scored leaf until an independently defined consequence is approved (an open owner decision). The shared-failure analysis of chapter 9 stays a table of its own, never a weighted leaf.

Three checks hold the allocation: every expenditure the documents name is allocated once; the perspective totals sum to the option's cost of supply; the baseline's cost less the option's cost equals the NPV saving. A one-off the documents give for one option only goes into the leaf its nature places it in (a bridge in logistics, a compensation measure in environment and permitting); the other options carry an evidenced zero there, or a justified range where the documents are silent. An expense that fits no leaf blocks the reconciliation check: the partition is then not complete, the map is not drawn from it, and the report says which expense is unplaced.

Five rules hold the scores:

  • Gates are not leaves. The rule's conditions are pass / fail / unknown per option, shown beside the map, never weighted.
  • A range stays a range. A leaf the files give as a range has a lower and an upper score bound, the direction applied correctly: for a cost the minimum raw value is the favourable end, so the map never draws it as the base. Both bound views are shown and labelled as bounds, not as jointly feasible futures. Nothing fills a missing fact, and a fact the client's file states is not widened into a range.
  • One expenditure, one leaf. NPV saving, after-tax NPV, IRR and payback are printed beside the weighted score, unweighted, because they already contain every leaf.
  • Judgement is labelled. A leaf whose value is a person's judgement carries that person's name and the grade úsudek.
  • An empty perspective stays and says why; a globally inactive perspective has weight zero for every option, approved as such. A nonzero weight on an empty perspective is refused with the reason; nothing is redistributed silently.

One scoring structure for every option

For an option i and a perspective p, Cip is the discounted sum of p's leaves. The approved value function vp maps it onto 0–10 on fixed anchors shared by all options; a value outside the anchors triggers a review, never silent clipping. The weighted score is

Si(w) = Σp wp · vp(Cip)

  • One weight vector for every option. No option loses a weight because a leaf does not apply to it: evidenced absence of an applicable expenditure is a cost of zero through the common scale; unknown expenditure is a range with two bounds. A rule under which a missing component "takes no weight there" would compare options under different trade-offs, and is not used.
  • Leaves are an accounting breakdown, not equally weighted subcriteria. Splitting a cost line (freight into rail and road) leaves the perspective total, the score and the rank unchanged. This is the admission test of every allocation; sharing a perspective's weight equally among its components fails it.
  • Anchors and swing weights are approved together, before any option's score is shown: a weight is a trade-off over a stated swing of a perspective's total, not a feeling about a category name. The additive form itself is reviewed with them.
  • A scenario is a named weight vector the client's people approve; the researcher records it with its scales through the existing approval flow (the owner, 9 November), with who composed it and when, before results.

An example, with the remaining weight assigned to finance: finance 0,20 · strategie 0 · provoz 0,50 · marketing 0 · logistika 0,30 · prostředí 0 · lidé 0. "Finance" is its partition, the capital, land and purchases, not the total NPV. The NPV still includes every cost, including the perspectives given zero diagnostic weight.

Ranges and sensitivity

A ranged leaf gives the option an interval of scores. A robust leader needs a validated positive lower bound on its score difference against every rival over all admitted unknowns jointly; overlapping intervals mean leadership is not established, and the map says so. For each named scenario the study tests, in this order and reported separately:

  1. the approved centre;
  2. Dirichlet fans at concentrations 20, 60 and 180, 300 settings each, drawn on the positive-weight coordinates only so an explicit zero stays zero;
  3. directed weight transfers between pairs of perspectives, and a simplex grid where the number of active perspectives keeps it small;
  4. the approved alternative anchors and the uncertain cost allocations;
  5. the evidence bounds, coherent joint stress cases and simultaneous unknowns.

Results are shares of tested settings, per scenario, per fan and per evidence setting: the share in which an option is uniquely first, the share of ties and the share left unresolved by the bounds, which together reconcile to 100 %. Scenarios are never pooled into one popularity figure.

The Sociomap view of a scenario

Each scenario has its own view on the aia-sociomap-5 engine, under the mode contract the methodologist registers for it (sociomapa-systemu#SYS12, SYS13):

  • rows are the tested weight settings; objects are the options; proximity is similarity of score response across the settings; height is the score under the selected setting;
  • a ranged leaf keeps its paired bound views and score intervals in the table beside the map;
  • the gate status of every option (pass / fail / unknown) is printed beside it; a hypothetical change of a gate's state is a named scenario, never an edit;
  • the rule's verdict stands in a panel of its own; the map is preference information under Co by závěr změnilo and never the recommendation;
  • no respondent labels, no "reliable pairs", no respondent segments and no sampling-error circles appear: those belong to the respondent mode, whose statistics this mode does not read;
  • the view records its layout fit, its numerical convergence and its sensitivity under the mode contract; geometry the contract does not support produces no spatial claim.
